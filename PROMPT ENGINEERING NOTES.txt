EMBEDDING MODELS 

Word2Vec -> A machine learning algorithm which generates a dense,distributed vector for a word. Ensures that similar words are placed together in the vector space.
	->Made from CBOW(Continuous bag of words) and Skip-gram.
	->(Trained on the entire English Wikipedia in 2013)

	->Embedded models consider the history and future of a CENTER WORD(previous words and next words), instead of predicting the next word like LLMs
	->SELF-SUPERVISED.(Data doesn't not require manual labeling)
	->No need for human labels.
	->is a SELF-SUPERVISED LEARNING ALGO, generates its OWN TRAINING LABELS from the structure of RAW TEXT WITHOUT HUMAN ANNOTATION.
	
	Hyperparametes
	->corpus size/source/type
	->context window
	->vector dimensions
	->context symmetry/position
	->sampling frequent words/delete rare words

	->RESULTING EMBEDDINGS ARE STATIC IN Word2vec.
	->vector will be decided ones and dont change(single,fixed (static) vector regardless of where it appears).
	->but vector will be different in a different context


CBOW -> Predicts w(t) , the TARGET WORD using previous words , W(t-1) W(t-2) and W(+1) W(t+2)

HOW DOES GLoVE work?	1->Preprocess the Text
				->First,we split the text into individual words(tokenization) so that we can work with them.
			2->Creating the Vocabulary
			3->Create the Co-Occurence matrix
			4->Performing Dot prduct ->aim is to learn word vectors such that the dot product of two word vectors reflects how often the words co-occur in the context.
			5->Training the Word Vectors ->The model optimizes the word vectors.

			The	0.3,0.1,0.5
			Peon	0.2,0.4,1.0
			
			->You make a Co-occurence matrix of every word wrt to its immediate meighbour(Context size=1)

PMI	->Pointwise Mutual Information (measure of how often two events x and y occur,compared with what we would expect if they were imdependent).
	->B/w a TARGET WORD W AND A CONTEXT WORD C is then: 
								PMI(w,c) = log2(P(w,c)/P(w)*P(c))
	PPMI is preferred over PMI in practical NLP system because it removes unreliable negative associations.

RECURRENT NEURAL NETWORKS (RNNs) (Sequential Modelling)

->RNNs process sequences one step at a time,maintaining a hidden state that caries information forward.

Sentence "The cat sat on the mat", when predicting the next word after "sat," the RNN uses previous word ("the Cat") to understand the context.
LIMITATION: When the sentence is long ("The cat that chased the house sat on the mat") , early words face - the vanishing dradient problem.
->This inspired LSTMs.

 h0 - > h1 -> h2 -> h3 -> h4
			.The final state h4 is called as the context vector which is idreally supposed to store the entire context of the sentence in a single vector.
->for huge inputs, the hidden state will not be able to store LONG RANGE DEPENDENCIES and hence, the decoder will not be able to translate well given only the previous hidden stte words.
->maintains something called a HIDDEN STATE.
->think of it as the network's current MEMORY.
